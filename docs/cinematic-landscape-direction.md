# Room to imagine — cinematic landscape direction

This revision supersedes the illustrated courtyard direction after the user supplied nature-film references. The desired atmosphere is dreamlike cinema: emerald slopes, small white birds, a white horse, silver water and a rose-lavender sunset. The first alpine candidate was rejected for adding mountains and excessive natural detail; three subsequent scenes follow the simpler supplied reference.

## The visual story

1. Introduction: useful software, room to imagine. A readable professional identity on deep forest green.
2. Three panoramic landscape frames: curiosity (birds), possibility (horse and water), perspective (dusk). The reference’s rounded film-frame rhythm becomes the visual identity.
3. Selected work: real project narratives and clearly authored architecture summaries. Atmospheric art is never presented as project evidence.
4. The curious part: user-started working sketch and links to the original experiments.
5. Field notes: readable editorial entries, followed by a closer shoreline crop as a quiet visual pause.
6. About and contact: personal context and a warmer pink accent in the closing invitation.

The later visual correction intentionally prioritizes cinematic atmosphere over the previous tight first-project fold target. Work and contact remain directly accessible in the header and hero.

## Motion and media

These are generated still images, not generated videos. No video source loads. The shoreline interlude has small native scroll-linked movement only where view timelines are supported; older browsers get the static frame. Reduced motion disables it. There is no continuous ambient animation, automatic sound or scroll hijacking. Image-to-video prompts are provided separately for a future loop.

Responsive Next Image variants serve three WebP masters. Scene captions label the imagined story; hero credit identifies the landscape as made for the portfolio. The earlier courtyard master remains in the branch history but is not used by the page.
